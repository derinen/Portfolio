01 / INTRODUCTION

A little about
where I'm coming from.

Hello, I'm Derin Enhoş, an MSc Construction Economics and Management student at University College London (UCL), with a BArch in Architecture from the University of Nottingham.

My interests lie in the commercial, economic and strategic aspects of the construction industry, particularly construction and project management, quantity surveying and consultancy. I am fascinated by construction economics, market dynamics and the business decisions shaping the industry. While my undergraduate background is in Architecture, my career interests are firmly focused on the management and commercial side of construction rather than architectural design.

Through internships in the UK, Turkey and the UAE, I've gained experience across architectural design, interior design, engineering and marketing.

Alongside my studies, I am a Sustainability Ambassador for UCL's BSSC Green Impact Builders. I'm passionate about weightlifting, windsurfing and gymnastics, and am a member of the UCL Gymnastics Team where I compete nationally against other universities. I also am a personal trainer holding a Level 3 Diploma from PureGym.

Having lived and studied internationally, I value diverse perspectives and enjoy connecting with people from different backgrounds. I'm always open to new opportunities, collaborations and conversations within the industry.

01 / RESEARCH

Extended Essay

Traces of the “Bauhaus” in the Şakirin Mosque

My 3,727-word IB Diploma research essay investigates the relationship between modernist principles and cultural identity in contemporary religious architecture.

“To what extent does the Şakirin Mosque follow the Bauhaus philosophy of ‘form follows function’?”

02 / VISUAL ARTS

IB Art Exhibition

Inspired by visits to Istanbul’s Blue Mosque and Şakirin Mosque, the exhibition explores the contrast between traditional architecture and the perceived sameness of modern design. Illusion-like architectural artworks express my unease at the loss of cultural identity.

Using oil paint, monochrome, charcoal, lino printing and plaster, I explored repetition through print and 3D relief. In Traces of Islam in the Şakirin, painted fragments of the mosque connect the visual work directly to my essay research.
